Powers

Powers are where you define what your character can do. If you want telepathy, super strength, summoning, flight, or almost anything you can think of, powers are what you need. Every power is composed of Effects that may be modified with various extras or flaws. The effects are quite general, so many different super human abilities can fall under the same effect. For instance, attacking with claws, a long range beam of ice, or an AoE cloud of fire, all fall under the Damage effect. The difference between them is the way they are described as well as the different Extras and Flaws that are applied to the Effect.

Types of Effects

Effects are divided into 6 different categories. Attack, Defense, Control, Movement, Sensory, and General. Effects of the same type follow similar rules and do similar things.

Attack

Attack effects are, as the name implies, effects that are used offensively and damage, hinder, or harm the target. The four attack effects are Affliction, Damage Nullify, and Weaken. They all require a standard action and an attack check to use, and they are always rolled against a resistance check by their target.

Defense

Defense effects all work to protect in different ways, granting bonuses to resistance checks or immunity to specific effects or conditions. The five defense effects are Deflect, Immunity, Protection, Regeneration, and Immortality. Most defense effects work only on the user and are subtle and permanent, functioning at all times. Some are activated and sustained as a free action, meaning they can switch on or off, but can potentially leave the user unprotected.

Control

Control effects allow the user to move, create, or change a variety of different things. The seven control effects are Environment, Illusion, Create, Move Object, Summon, Transform, and Luck Control. Most take a standard action to initiate and can be sustained. Control effects and the hazards they can cause usually require an attack check and allow for a resistance check when used on an unwilling target.

Movement

Movement effects enhance existing methods of movement or provide different ways for their users to get around. The seven movement effects are Burrowing, Leaping, Speed, Swimming, Flight, Movement, and Teleport. Most of them are labeled as a free action but only work to modify a move action.

Sensory

Sensory effects enhance or alter the senses. the six sensory effects are Senses (a large umbrella effect), Remote Sensing, Comprehend, Concealment, Mind Reading, and Communication. They are typically a free action to use, or permanent.
The basic senses types referred to in Sensory effects are Visual, Auditory, Olfactory, Tactile, and Mental. There are other special types such as magnetism, cosmic, magic, or radio which are less common.
You may notice the language of some effects refer to a single sense or to a sense type, senses are things like x-ray vision or night vision, and both these examples would fall under the sense type of vision.

General

General effects is a catch all category for effects that dont fit in to the other groups and as such, the largest. There are eleven general effects: Elongation, Extra Limbs, Feature, Quickness, Growth, Healing, Shrinking, Insubstantial, Morph, Variable, and Enhanced Trait. There aren’t any overarching rules for the General category

Additional Effect Info

Effect Checks

Effect checks are occasionally required to determine how well the effect works. These checks are done as any other, 1d20+rank+modifiers against the DC, unless they are done as an Opposed check against another effect or a particular trait.

Effect Parameters

All effects have parameters that describe the action needed to use it, the range it works at, and how long it works.
Actions are pretty self-explanatory and keep relatively close to pathfinder rules so I will not go over them

Range

  • Personal: The effect works only on the user.
  • Close: The effect can target anyone or anything the user is touching. Touching an unwilling subject requires an unarmed attack check against the subject’s Parry.
  • Ranged: The effect works at a distance, limited by perception and path and requiring a ranged attack check against the subject’s Dodge defense.
    • short range (rank x 25 feet)
    • medium range (rank x 50 feet) and a -2 circumstance penalty
    • long range of (rank x 100 feet) and a -5 circumstance penalty
  • Perception: The effect works on any target you can perceive with an accurate sense, without any need for an attack check.
  • Rank: The effect’s range or area of effect is determined by its rank, as given in its description.

Duration

  • Instant: When used, the effect occurs and ends in the same turn, although its results may linger.
  • Concentration: You can keep a concentration effect going by taking a standard action each round to do so. If you are incapable of taking the necessary action, or simply choose not to, the effect ends.
  • Sustained: You can keep a sustained effect going by taking a free action each round to do so. If you are incapable of taking the necessary action, or simply choose not to, the effect ends.
  • Continuous: The effect lasts as long as you wish, without any action required on your part. Once active, it stays that way until you choose to deactivate it (a free action).
  • Permanent: The effect is always active and cannot be deactivated, even if you want to. A permanent effect cannot be improved using extra effort.

Modifiers

Modifiers are how you can change effects, either enhancing them with Extras or giving them limits or weaknesses with Flaws. Extras will increase to the cost of a power, either by adding to the per rank cost or adding a flat additional cost. Flaws will decrease the cost of a power in much the same way.

Partial Modifiers

Modifiers can be applied to only some ranks of an effect to fine tune the effect. for example you could modify a damage 7 effect so that it’s first 4 ranks are AoE, this would mean on a direct hit the target would have to resist the full damage 7 but on a near miss they would still have to resist the area damage 4

    • Action: Standard
    • Range: Close
    • Duration: Instant
    • Cost: 1 per rank

    You can impose some debilitating condition or conditions on a target.
    You set the conditions your Affliction causes at each degree when you acquire it and they may not be changed.

    • Success: No Effect
    • 1 Degree of Failure: The target is dazed, entranced, fatigued, hindered, impaired, or vulnerable.
    • 2 Degrees of Failure: The target is compelled, defenseless, disabled, exhausted, immobile, prone, or stunned.
    • 3 Degrees of Failure: The target is asleep, controlled, incapacitated, paralyzed, transformed or unaware

    The target of an Affliction makes a resistance check at the end of each of his turns to remove first and second degree conditions. Third degree conditions require a minute of recovery time or outside aid, such as the Treatment skill or Healing effect (DC 10 + rank).
    The exact nature and descriptors of the Affliction are up to you, chosen when you acquire the effect, with the GM’s approval.
